# Why our month-end close went from nine days to four

## Day nine, March

In March last year our close finished on the ninth working day. The board pack went out on the eleventh, two days after the budget meeting it was meant for. Our finance lead, Aoife, rebuilt the cash forecast by hand that weekend because the numbers in the pack were already out of date.

## Where the days went

We timed every step for three closes. Bank reconciliations took a day and a half, mostly because two of our five accounts still came in as PDF statements. Supplier accruals took two days: 140 open purchase orders, and nobody owned the list. Intercompany with the Belfast entity took most of a day, every month, for a balance that was usually under 2,000 euro.

Payroll journals were fine. They took an hour.

## What we changed

We moved both PDF accounts to bank feeds in April, which cost nothing but a form at each bank. Accruals now close against a purchase order report that the ops team reviews on the 25th, so most of the list is cleared before month end. For intercompany we agreed a fixed monthly recharge with the Belfast team and settle the true-up each quarter instead of each month.

Three changes, no new software.

## What did not work

We tried a close checklist in a shared spreadsheet first (May and June). It made the late steps visible, which helped in meetings; it did not make them faster, and by June half the rows were ticked on the day of the review rather than the day the work was done. We dropped it. The bank feeds and the purchase order report did more in one month than the checklist did in two.

## Four days, and what is still slow

Since July the close has finished on day four, and the board pack goes out on day six. Fixed assets is now the slowest step, because the register still lives in a spreadsheet that one person updates. That is the next thing we will fix, probably in the first quarter.

If your close runs past day five, time each step for one month before you buy anything. Ours had two slow steps and they were not the ones we guessed.
